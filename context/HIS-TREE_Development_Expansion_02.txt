==================================================
1. THE BIGGER VISION
==================================================

HIS-TREE must become an immersive 3D historical time-travel experience.

The player should not simply read about historical events, walk around historical locations or listen to NPCs explain what happened.

THE PLAYER MUST PARTICIPATE IN HISTORY.

For example, when experiencing a World War campaign, the player should be able to:

- Join a military formation.
- March with other soldiers.
- Receive mission instructions.
- Move through historically appropriate environments.
- Experience battlefield conditions.
- Take part in appropriate combat missions.
- Use historically appropriate equipment.
- Complete objectives.
- Experience the consequences of decisions.
- Progress through the historical timeline.

The same principle applies to African history and biblical experiences.

Every major historical event should be represented as a CAMPAIGN containing multiple connected missions.

A campaign is not one isolated level.

It should tell the story of an event from its beginning through its important developments and conclusion.

The player should experience the event in chronological order.

The objective is to make history playable.

==================================================
2. TWO DISTINCT HISTORICAL GAMEPLAY MODES
==================================================

Preserve the two modes from the original specification.

A. HISTORICAL MODE

This mode allows players to participate in actual historical events.

The player follows the documented historical timeline.

The player can participate in missions, explore environments, interact with historical figures and experience the circumstances surrounding important events.

The actual historical outcome must remain historically accurate.

B. REWRITE HISTORY MODE

This mode allows players to attempt to change historical outcomes.

For example:
- Prevent Abraham Lincoln's assassination.
- Change the outcome of a fictionalised alternate-history military campaign.
- Influence decisions that affect the development of an empire.

The player makes decisions and experiences alternative consequences.

Every changed outcome must be labelled ALTERNATE HISTORY.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION:

Historical Mode = Experience what actually happened.

Rewrite History Mode = Explore what might have happened differently.

Do not confuse these two modes.

Biblical episodes are different.

BIBLICAL MODE MUST NOT HAVE REWRITE HISTORY.

The biblical campaigns are intended to let players experience and learn the events recorded in Scripture.

CAMPAIGN 1: THE ABA WOMEN'S WAR — 1929
--------------------------------------------------

LOCATION:
Colonial south-eastern Nigeria.

HISTORICAL EVENT:
The Aba Women's War, also known as the Women's War of 1929.

CORE EXPERIENCE:

The player experiences the historical protests led by Igbo and other women against colonial policies and the warrant chief system.

This campaign should communicate the scale, organisation, courage and historical significance of the women's resistance.

POSSIBLE MISSION STRUCTURE:

MISSION 1 — LIFE UNDER COLONIAL RULE

Introduce the player to the historical setting.

Reconstruct a Nigerian community during the colonial period.

Show:
- Traditional compounds.
- Marketplaces.
- Community life.
- Women's economic activities.
- Traditional clothing.
- Colonial administrative structures.

Allow the player to interact with residents and understand the conditions that led to the protests.

MISSION 2 — THE GRIEVANCES

Introduce the colonial taxation controversy and the concerns surrounding warrant chiefs and colonial administration.

The player gathers information and learns why women were becoming increasingly concerned.

MISSION 3 — THE WOMEN MOBILISE

The player participates in the organisation and communication surrounding the protests.

Show women gathering, communicating, singing, marching and coordinating their actions.

MISSION 4 — THE PROTESTS

The player participates in historically grounded protest activities.

Reconstruct the atmosphere of the demonstrations and the interactions with colonial authorities.

MISSION 5 — THE CONSEQUENCES

Show the consequences of the protests, the colonial response, and the historical significance of the movement.

GAMEPLAY REQUIREMENTS:

- Crowd simulation appropriate to the available resources.
- NPC conversations.
- Group movement.
- Mission objectives.
- Historically appropriate clothing.
- Accurate environmental design.
- Period-appropriate architecture.
- Cultural details informed by historical research.

IMPORTANT:

Do not turn the Aba Women's War into a generic shooting or combat game.

The central experience is organised resistance, protest, community mobilisation and the struggle against colonial injustice.

Respect the women and communities involved.

--------------------------------------------------

LOCATION:
Nigeria.

HISTORICAL EVENT:
The death of General Sani Abacha on 8 June 1998.

This campaign should explore the political and historical circumstances surrounding the end of Abacha's rule and the transition that followed.

Do not automatically describe Abacha's death as an assassination.

The circumstances and reported cause of his death should be researched using credible historical sources.

MISSION 1 — NIGERIA UNDER MILITARY RULE

Reconstruct the political and social atmosphere of Nigeria during the Abacha administration.

MISSION 2 — THE PRESIDENCY

Introduce the historical presidential environment, military administration and key institutions.

MISSION 3 — THE FINAL DAYS

Explore the documented events leading up to Abacha's death.

Use credible research to establish what can actually be represented.

MISSION 4 — THE ANNOUNCEMENT

The player experiences the historical announcement and the national reaction.

MISSION 5 — THE TRANSITION

Explore the political developments that followed his death and the eventual transition towards civilian government.

VISUAL REQUIREMENTS:

- Period-appropriate Nigerian architecture.
- Government buildings.
- Military uniforms.
- Vehicles.
- Street environments.
- Communication technology.
- News and media environments.
- Period-appropriate clothing.

Do not use modern Nigerian environments or technology where they would be historically inappropriate.

BIBLICAL EXPERIENCES

5. BIBLICAL VISUAL REALISM — A MAJOR REQUIREMENT
==================================================

The biblical episodes must aim for the highest visual realism achievable within the existing development environment.

I do not want cartoonish characters, fantasy armour, generic medieval environments or exaggerated video-game representations.

The target is:

CINEMATIC, HISTORICALLY INFORMED, HIGH-FIDELITY 3D REALISM.

Think of a high-quality historical film recreated as an interactive 3D game.

The coding agent is text-based and may not be able to inspect images generated separately.

Therefore, use the detailed descriptions in this document as the visual direction.

Use all available technical resources to achieve the best possible result.

Do not simply select the first available 3D model and place it in the environment.

Search the asset library, inspect models, remodel assets, create custom materials and use Blender where necessary.

Use the existing Blender and Three.js workflow.

A. CHARACTER REALISM

Characters should have:
- Realistic human proportions.
- Natural body movement.
- Period-appropriate hairstyles.
- Period-appropriate facial hair.
- Historically informed skin and clothing textures.
- Natural-looking faces.
- Realistic clothing folds.
- Believable walking and running animations.
- Appropriate body language.
- Natural interaction animations.

Avoid:
- Cartoon proportions.
- Oversized heads.
- Modern hairstyles.
- Modern clothing.
- Fantasy armour.
- Plastic-looking skin.
- Unnaturally shiny materials.

Where high-quality realistic human models are available, use them.

Where necessary, use Blender to remodel and improve available models.

B. CLOTHING AND COSTUMES

Research the clothing of the relevant biblical period.

Do not dress every character in generic robes.

Clothing should reflect:
- Historical period.
- Geographic location.
- Social position.
- Occupation.
- Wealth.
- Cultural background.

Use realistic woven fabric materials, natural colours, appropriate sandals, belts, head coverings and other historically informed details.

C. ENVIRONMENT REALISM

Reconstruct environments using historically informed architecture and materials.

Consider:
- Mud-brick structures.
- Stone buildings.
- Ancient settlements.
- Desert landscapes.
- Rocky terrain.
- River environments.
- Agricultural land.
- Ancient roads.
- Wooden structures.
- Natural vegetation.

Use realistic terrain, atmospheric effects, lighting, shadows, sky, water and environmental sound.

Do not introduce modern buildings, roads, objects or materials.

D. CINEMATIC LIGHTING AND ATMOSPHERE

Lighting is essential.

Use appropriate:
- Sunrise and sunset lighting.
- Harsh desert sunlight.
- Night-time illumination.
- Firelight.
- Torchlight.
- Dust and atmospheric particles.
- Shadows.
- Environmental fog where appropriate.
- Realistic water and reflections.

The environment should change according to the time of day and mission.

E. SOUND AND MUSIC

Audio must contribute to the historical atmosphere.

Use:
- Period-inspired instrumental music.
- Environmental ambience.
- Wind.
- Footsteps.
- Animal sounds.
- Market sounds.
- Crowd sounds.
- Water.
- Fire.
- Battle ambience where appropriate.
- Appropriate musical transitions.

Avoid modern electronic music in biblical episodes.

Use music that is historically informed and appropriate to the setting.

Do not claim that a modern musical reconstruction is the exact music used in biblical times.

==================================================

BIBLICAL SOURCE:
Exodus 13–15.

SETTING:
Ancient Egypt and the wilderness journey of the Israelites.

CORE EXPERIENCE:

This should be one of the most visually impressive experiences in HIS-TREE.

The player should experience the events leading up to the crossing, the Israelites' situation, Pharaoh's pursuit, the opening of the sea and the crossing.

POSSIBLE MISSION STRUCTURE:

MISSION 1 — EGYPT

Reconstruct ancient Egypt.

Show:
- Egyptian architecture.
- Settlements.
- Palace environments.
- Ancient Egyptian clothing.
- Egyptian military equipment.
- Everyday life.
- The Israelites' circumstances.

MISSION 2 — THE EXODUS

Show the Israelites leaving Egypt.

Create a large travelling population.

The player participates in the journey.

MISSION 3 — THE WILDERNESS

Reconstruct the environment and challenges of the journey.

Show the large population travelling through the wilderness.

MISSION 4 — PHARAOH'S PURSUIT

Present the biblical account of Pharaoh's army pursuing the Israelites.

Create a cinematic and tense sequence.

MISSION 5 — THE SEA BEFORE THEM

The player reaches the sea with the Israelites.

Show the scale of the water, the surrounding landscape and the approaching Egyptian forces.

MISSION 6 — THE SEA OPENS

Create an extraordinary cinematic representation of the biblical miracle.

The sea should separate into two enormous bodies of water.

The player should witness the scale and physical environment of the divided sea.

Create:
- Massive walls of water.
- A visible passage through the sea.
- A seabed pathway.
- Water movement and environmental effects.
- Lighting through the divided water.
- The sound of powerful moving water.
- A large crowd beginning the crossing.

MISSION 7 — THE CROSSING

Allow the player to participate in the Israelites' journey through the divided sea.

Create a sense of scale.

Show families, groups, possessions and animals travelling through the passage.

MISSION 8 — PHARAOH'S ARMY

Present the Egyptian army entering the passage.

MISSION 9 — THE WATERS RETURN

Present the biblical conclusion of the event as recorded in Exodus.

The waters return and Pharaoh's pursuing forces are overwhelmed.

MISSION 10 — DELIVERANCE

Complete the campaign with the Israelites reaching safety.

Use Scripture-based narration and appropriate musical atmosphere.

IMPORTANT VISUAL REQUIREMENT:

The Red Sea crossing must not look like a small swimming pool with two walls of water.

It should feel enormous.

Use the largest visually convincing environment possible within browser performance limits.

If rendering an enormous environment simultaneously is too expensive, use carefully designed camera sequences, environmental streaming, level sections and cinematic transitions.

Maintain player participation.

Do not turn the entire episode into a movie.

- Biblical book.
- Chapter.

- Add alternative biblical endings.
