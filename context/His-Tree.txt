HIS-TREE

Experience History. Rewrite Destiny.

You are an autonomous senior game developer, 3D game designer, historical researcher, technical artist, and gameplay engineer.

Your task is to build HIS-TREE, a 3D browser-based historical time-travel adventure game, using the existing development environment and technical resources described in the supplied project context.

Do not build a simple history quiz, a collection of text pages, or a static educational experience. Build an immersive, playable 3D game in which players travel through historical periods, explore reconstructed environments, interact with people, complete missions, make decisions, and experience the consequences of their actions.

The defining idea is:

What actually happened? And what if history had happened differently?

The game must have two distinct gameplay modes.

---

1. GAME TITLE, PITCH AND GENRE

Title: HIS-TREE
First Episode: THE GOLDEN EMPIRE
Tagline: Experience History. Rewrite Destiny.

Genre:

- 3D historical adventure.
- Time-travel exploration.
- Mission-based gameplay.
- Historical simulation.
- Alternate-history strategy.
- Educational entertainment.

Core pitch:

His-Tree is a time-travel adventure that allows players to enter important moments in human history, explore the people and places involved, complete historically grounded missions, and discover how decisions shaped the world.

Players can experience events as they happened or activate Rewrite History Mode to attempt to change their outcomes.

The game should make history feel like something players can experience rather than simply read about.

2. PLAYER FANTASY

The player is a time traveller with access to different historical periods.

They can:

- Travel between historical eras.
- Explore historically inspired 3D environments.
- Meet historical figures.
- Interact with fictional characters based on people and occupations from the period.
- Discover historical objects and locations.
- Complete missions and challenges.
- Investigate historical events.
- Make decisions that influence events.
- Experience alternative outcomes.
- Compare actual history with the history they created.

The player should feel like an active participant in history.

Two gameplay modes

Every episode should support two modes, subject to the practical scope of the episode.

A. HISTORICAL MODE

The player experiences documented history.

Events, timelines, locations, and outcomes should be based on credible historical research.

The player may investigate, explore, assist people, solve problems, and complete missions within the historical setting.

However, the game must not falsely present changed historical outcomes as real events.

B. REWRITE HISTORY MODE

This is the larger-than-life mode.

The player is allowed to attempt to change history.

They can prevent events, alter decisions, influence important people, change military strategies, protect individuals, redirect resources, and create alternative outcomes.

The player should experience the consequences of changing history.

For example:

- Preventing an assassination could change the political future of a country.
- Changing a military strategy could produce a different battlefield outcome.
- Preventing a major disaster could create new challenges and consequences.
- Changing a ruler's economic decisions could alter the development of an empire.

This mode must be clearly labelled ALTERNATE HISTORY. Its events and outcomes are fictional possibilities, not claims about what actually happened.

The player should be able to switch between the two modes from the episode selection or time-travel hub.

3. CORE GAMEPLAY LOOP

The main gameplay loop is:

1. Enter the time-travel hub.
2. Select a historical period or episode.
3. Choose Historical Mode or Rewrite History Mode.
4. Arrive in the historical environment.
5. Explore the world.
6. Meet characters and gather information.
7. Receive missions and objectives.
8. Interact with the environment and complete challenges.
9. Make decisions where applicable.
10. Witness the consequences.
11. Unlock historical discoveries and journal entries.
12. Complete the episode.
13. Return to the time-travel hub and choose another experience.

The game should reward exploration, curiosity, problem-solving, and replayability.

4. MOMENT-TO-MOMENT GAMEPLAY

The player should be able to:

- Walk, run, jump, and explore.
- Interact with NPCs.
- Start conversations.
- Pick up and inspect selected objects.
- Activate mechanisms.
- Collect clues and historical artefacts.
- Complete puzzles.
- Follow mission objectives.
- Make dialogue and strategy decisions.
- Unlock new areas.
- Receive mission updates.
- View historical discoveries in a journal.

Rewrite History Mode should introduce additional mechanics where appropriate:

- Timed interventions.
- Decision points.
- Protection and rescue objectives.
- Strategy planning.
- Resource allocation.
- Branching missions.
- Consequence tracking.
- Alternative endings.

Do not make every episode a combat game. Each historical period should have mechanics that suit its setting.

10. REWRITE HISTORY: EXAMPLE CAMPAIGNS

This system is one of the most important features of His-Tree.

Do not treat Rewrite History as a simple dialogue option that changes a sentence. It should create meaningful alternative missions and consequences.

Example A: SAVE LINCOLN

Historical event: Abraham Lincoln's assassination on 14 April 1865.

Historical Mode:
The player investigates and experiences the historical circumstances surrounding the assassination, with the documented outcome preserved.

Rewrite History Mode:

Objective: Prevent the assassination.

Possible mission sequence:

1. Arrive in Washington, D.C., in April 1865.
2. Gather information about the assassination conspiracy.
3. Discover John Wilkes Booth's plan.
4. Investigate the locations and movements involved.
5. Protect Lincoln before the assassination takes place.
6. Prevent Booth from carrying out the attack.
7. Complete the intervention mission.
8. Experience the alternative political consequences of Lincoln surviving.

The player should have to investigate and make decisions rather than simply press a button to save Lincoln.

Possible outcomes:

- Lincoln survives.
- The attempt is discovered but the player fails to prevent every consequence.
- The player fails and the historical outcome remains unchanged.

Any political developments after Lincoln's survival must be clearly identified as fictional alternate history.

Example B: ALTERNATE WORLD WAR II MILITARY CAMPAIGN

Create a fictional alternate-history campaign inspired by World War II.

For example, the player takes command of German forces in a fictional scenario against British forces and attempts to achieve a military victory through strategy.

The player should be able to:

- Study the battlefield.
- Review available resources.
- Plan troop movements.
- Choose defensive or offensive strategies.
- Manage limited supplies.
- Make tactical decisions.
- Respond to changing battlefield conditions.
- Experience the consequences of strategic choices.

This is an alternate-history strategy scenario, not a claim that Germany defeated Britain in actual history.

Important:
The game must not glorify Nazi ideology, antisemitism, genocide, or war crimes. It must not turn historical atrocities into entertainment rewards. The military scenario should focus on fictional strategic decision-making and clearly distinguish the alternative scenario from documented history.

If the game explores a hypothetical World War III, it must use fictional future factions and explicitly label the scenario as speculative.

Example C: CHANGE THE COURSE OF AN EMPIRE

In the Mali episode, the player can influence trade, diplomacy, education, and infrastructure.

The player should see how different decisions create different fictional developments for the empire.

THE CONSEQUENCE SYSTEM

Build a reusable system that records major player decisions.

For example:

- Historical event: Lincoln was assassinated.
- Player intervention: Lincoln was protected.
- New outcome: Lincoln survived.
- Historical status: Fictional alternate outcome.

In later versions, introduce a Butterfly Effect System.

This system allows one major decision to influence later missions, characters, environments, and events.

Do not attempt to simulate every possible consequence in the first release. Begin with a few meaningful branches.

11. HISTORICAL JOURNAL AND INFORMATION SYSTEM

The player should have an in-game journal.

It should record:

- Historical discoveries.
- Important people.
- Locations.
- Artefacts.
- Mission achievements.
- Decisions made.
- Alternative outcomes.

Every historical entry must distinguish between:

- VERIFIED HISTORY: Supported by credible historical evidence.
- HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION: A reasonable interpretation of historical evidence.
- DISPUTED HISTORY: An account or detail where historians disagree.
- FICTIONAL CHARACTER: A character created for gameplay.
- FICTIONALISED DIALOGUE: Dialogue written for the game, not a verified quotation.
- ALTERNATE HISTORY: An outcome created by the player's actions.

Historical Mode must preserve documented historical outcomes.

Rewrite History Mode must identify what the player changed and what is fictional.

Use credible sources for historical research. Prioritise museums, archives, universities, academic publications, and reputable historical institutions.

Do not invent dates, quotations, people, or events and present them as facts.

RISKS TO MANAGE

Scope creep: Do not attempt to build ten historical episodes at once.

Performance: Optimise for browser performance and the available VM resources.

Historical accuracy: Research before implementing historical claims.

Branching complexity: Start with limited meaningful alternatives.

Player confusion: Clearly distinguish documented history from fictional outcomes.

Asset limitations: Remodel existing assets instead of attempting to create everything from scratch.

Narrative quality: Make missions engaging and playable, not just information delivery.

Sensitivity: Treat real-world tragedies, wars, slavery, genocide, terrorism, and victims with historical seriousness. Do not trivialise suffering or turn atrocities into spectacle.

FINAL DESIGN PRINCIPLE

His-Tree should make players curious about history.

A player should be able to enter an era, walk through its world, meet its people, understand what happened, and participate in meaningful missions.

Then they should be able to return to that same moment and ask:

What if I changed something?

Historical Mode answers:

WHAT ACTUALLY HAPPENED?

Rewrite History Mode answers:

WHAT IF THINGS HAD HAPPENED DIFFERENTLY?

Both experiences are essential to the identity of His-Tree.
